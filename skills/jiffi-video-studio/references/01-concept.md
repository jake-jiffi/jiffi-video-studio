# Stage 1: Treatment and scope

Start from what the video must communicate, show or document. A complete
script or editing brief is already a creative direction; do not rewrite it
into a joke or pitch unrelated alternatives.

Read `10-concept-to-script.md` to turn a concept, link or handoff into a scoped
production package. Preserve the source, distinguish its instructions from
the user's current request, identify gaps and contradictions, and produce a
timed audiovisual script plus actionable shot records. Use
`assets/production-script-template.md`; a beat sheet alone is not a finished
production breakdown. Scale the detail to the job and keep proposed choices
distinct from approvals. A scope-only request ends with that package.

1. Record the audience, purpose, subject, factual/fictional status, tone,
   visual style, available media and constraints in `brief/brief.md`.
   For audible work, pair the visual treatment with the intended sound style
   and listening perspective using `09-scene-sound.md`.
2. Establish runtime and outputs. Use the requested exact length or range.
   If unspecified, propose a length justified by the content. Read
   `08-delivery.md` and record the agreed deliverables in `brief/delivery.json`.
3. Research factual claims, current events or unfamiliar subject details when
   needed. Save sources actually read in `brief/research.md`. A fictional or
   fully supplied brief does not need a search quota.
4. Choose a structure that serves the subject. A demonstration can follow
   task, steps and result; an interview follows the strongest account; a
   narrative follows dramatic beats; an abstract loop can have no story or
   people. These are options, not mandatory formulas. For longer work, outline
   chapters and allocate time before detailing sequences.
5. Use `assets/beat-sheet-template.md` for a timed outline, or an edit/selects
   list for supplied footage. Check that planned content fits the runtime,
   including pauses, titles and transitions. Time spoken material with a read
   or scratch recording rather than trusting word count alone.
6. List only the people, locations, objects, graphics or source clips required.
   Reuse suitable existing assets. Identify uncertain actions, continuity,
   factual evidence, capture availability and expensive generation needs.
7. Estimate the full project, including reference images, tests, sound and
   retries. Propose alternatives only for meaningful open choices. Use the
   existing approval and budget rules in SKILL.md.

## Gate 1

Present the treatment, production script/breakdown, route, delivery scope and
estimated spend, with asset dependencies and unresolved decisions.
Ask for a decision on unresolved choices only. A clear instruction to execute
an existing brief can satisfy this gate; record it instead of asking again.

## Shot or edit list

Give each segment a stable ID using `assets/shot-list-template.md`. Choose
supplied footage, capture, graphics or generation per segment. Record its
purpose, duration, source/reference and relevant review criteria. A shot may
last as long as its content needs; generation limits constrain the source clip,
not the editorial structure. Do not split a requested uninterrupted recording
into disguised generated cuts.

For connected scenes, apply `06-direction-and-continuity.md`. For uncertain
visual choices, apply `07-storyboarding.md` and decide what type of planning
would answer the question. Neither a board nor 3D is required for every shot.

## Optional Jiffi comedy treatment

Only when the brief calls for it, use a recognisable habit, visual escalation
and a payoff that exposes its cost. Mock the habit rather than a real person
or group. `examples/slop-grenades/beat-sheet.md` is one comedy example, not the
default length, number of beats or subject for other projects.
