# Sound that belongs to the picture

Use when a project has dialogue, effects or ambience. Plan sound with the
visual treatment and scene layout, before generating performances. For simple
work, record the relevant choices in the brief. For several scenes or changing
perspectives, use `assets/sound-plan-template.md` as `brief/sound-plan.md`.
Silent projects can mark this not applicable. This adds no approval gate.

For a soundtrack's emotional arc and music choices, use
[Sound bible and spotting](11-sound-bible-and-spotting.md). A sound bible records
the project's creative rules; a spotting pass decides where each cue belongs
and why. Both can live in the existing sound plan.

## Separate identity, performance and space

Keep a recurring character's voice source/preset and defining vocal qualities
with their visual identity in the cast registry. Use the brief and audition
to choose language, accent, timbre and habitual delivery. Do not infer an
accent or personality solely from appearance. Keep an accepted reference line
when useful and already available. A change of room does not need a new voice.

Record performance per shot: intention, emotional state, physical effort,
breathing, pace and vocal projection. It should agree with the visible acting
and circumstances. An exhausted delivery or calling across a space needs an
appropriate performance; a volume change or reverb cannot create one. Preserve
the content and character of supplied real performances.

Record the scene's listening perspective separately. Identify sounds that
exist in the scene, sounds heard through a device, narration and music. Clean
narration need not inherit the room shown beneath it. A close-miked interview
can have clear dialogue even in a busy location. Use the intended style and
recording perspective rather than degrading good sound to simulate realism.

## Describe the acoustic setting

For relevant scenes, connect the sound plan to the same scene and shot IDs as
the visual plan. Specify:

- Enclosed or open space, apparent scale and important hard/soft surfaces.
- Subject-to-listener or microphone distance, orientation, movement and any
  obstruction, doorway or device affecting what is heard.
- Persistent ambience justified by the setting, plus distinct audible events
  and their timing. Do not add stock wildlife, traffic or weather arbitrarily.
- The intended relationship between voice, reflections, ambience and effects.
  Use a representative location recording as a reference when available.
- Changes that the action should cause: a door closes, a source moves away,
  a machine stops, or an object contacts a different surface.

Treat these as a reasoned sound design, not an exact acoustic measurement from
an image. Open air does not automatically mean echo, and a large room does
not justify a fixed heavy reverb preset. A tighter crop or different lens does
not itself move the listener. Keep dialogue perspective coherent across cuts
unless the action or chosen sound design motivates a change. Avoid panning
voices back and forth merely to follow their screen position after each cut.

## Build and match the mix

Keep original voice recordings and separate editable dialogue, ambience,
effects and music tracks where available. A dry generated voice is useful
source material; it is not automatically a finished scene mix.

First select a believable performance and align it to the picture. Then use
the existing editor's verified controls for level, tonal balance, reflections
or reverb and spatial placement as needed. Match replacement dialogue to the
surrounding takes and intended space. Do not add the same reverb to every line,
or stack new room sound on already reverberant source audio without listening.
Inspect the available Resolve/Fairlight controls before promising a particular
effect. This guidance does not authorise installing another audio product.

Use a consistent acoustic treatment across a continuous setting, with motivated
changes for distance, movement and obstructions. Keep room tone or ambience
continuous through edits and speech pauses where the setting calls for it.
Avoid obvious loop repeats, abrupt noise-floor jumps or a background that gates
on only while someone speaks. Crossfade or overlap ambience where appropriate.

Match effects to visible material, scale, contact, motion and distance. Align
audible events with their visual causes. An action that changes the world may
need a changed sound afterwards, not just an impact at the moment of contact.
For factual material, do not invent sound presented as evidence of a real event.

Preserve intelligibility, headroom and the delivery's loudness requirements.
Do not bury speech under noise, exaggerate echo or add clipping/wind distortion
just to make it sound less like a studio. If the effect needed for the scene
cannot be produced or assessed with available tools, record the limitation.

## Listen against the image and across cuts

Before applying a treatment throughout a project, review a representative
passage with picture: a line, its pause, a relevant action and an adjacent cut.
Use existing material for this check when possible. Compare the original and
treated passage at similar perceived loudness so louder does not mean better.
Inspect dialogue alone for edit problems, then judge placement in the full mix.

Check that voice identity and delivery suit the character and performance;
distance and space sound plausible from the intended listening perspective;
effects follow visible events; and ambience survives pauses and cuts naturally.
Listen on the intended playback route, with a mono check where relevant.

Reject detached studio-sounding replacement dialogue where the scene requires
audible placement, unexplained acoustic changes, duplicated native/replacement
speech, mismatched effort, or treatment that masks the words. Clear studio-like
narration, an intentional sound contrast or a supported microphone perspective
can be correct. Record those choices rather than enforcing one realism preset.

Add a sound-to-picture verdict and playback evidence to `export/review.md`,
covering the exact final files. Keep unverified listening unverified. Waveforms,
transcripts, codecs and level measurements cannot establish acoustic realism.

## Background

[iZotope's dialogue-matching documentation](https://downloads.izotope.com/docs/dialogue-match/en/index.html)
describes matching tonal balance, reverb and ambience across production and
replacement dialogue. This supports evaluating those properties together;
it is not a recommendation to buy that product or evidence our mix is fixed.
The scene planning and review decisions above apply that principle to this
studio's existing workflow.

For an available model that accepts actual audio/video bytes, use
[Audio and video review](12-audio-video-review.md). Its report is attributed
model analysis, not direct hearing by the calling agent. Verify factual claims
against source tracks and user feedback before changing accepted sound.

## Music direction and relative level

When music matters to the brief, establish its dramatic job before choosing a
track: tension, relief, momentum, atmosphere or an intentional comic accent.
For a brand with existing films, inspect supplied examples and record useful
music references. Distinguish a direct listening observation from a track
credit, prompt intention or inference. “Original audio” on a social post does
not identify its embedded music, prove a licence or establish a brand genre.
Use public posts as references only unless reuse rights are established.

Do not let the genre of the opening automatically determine the music for a
product section. A dry comedy may need a calm, confident transition. Other
concepts may need energetic or comic music. Make that choice from the brief
and references, rather than enforcing one style across every project.

Check each music entrance, the preceding quiet passage and the dialogue at a
consistent playback volume. Measure section loudness and short-term behaviour
as well as the full programme. A whole-video target can hide a music bed that
jumps to foreground level. Use measurements to choose an initial balance, then
judge musical fit, dynamics and the ending through playback. There is no
universal gain reduction or LUFS target for every cue.

If the tools cannot relay sound for listening, say so before claiming an
audition or source-matched music choice. Ask for a named track or a useful
musical description, or present a clearly labelled proposal within the existing
review gate. A quieter version of a rejected cue is only a level comparison;
it does not resolve the rejected mood. Record both outcomes separately and
carry an audience music rejection forward even when prior picture was accepted.
